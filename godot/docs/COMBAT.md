# Hollowmere combat

Maren is the prey. She is a diver in a lead-weighted suit on an island the sea has got into, and everything that
lives here was here first. Combat should feel like surviving an encounter, not winning one: the drowned close on her
faster than she can comfortably back away, a fight costs ammunition she has to scrape together, and the Mere's
Hollow is a last breath, not a weapon. The legendary weapons of the last night (Act III) are strong, and each one
asks for something in return.

## The drowned

- **Shuckers** walk at 55 px/s (85 crawling). Once one has seen her inside eight tiles it **lurches**: a scrabbling
  burst at 118 px/s (135 crawling) for most of a second, nearly as fast as she runs, then it must stop and gather
  itself for one to two seconds before the next. Sneaking (70) and reloading (locked) are no longer safe at mid range.
- Their wind-up is 0.5 s (from 0.6), the lunge 250 px/s (from 200), and a grab bites every second (from 1.3).
- Their limbs hold 4 (from 3) and the crown 5 (from 4): two rivets a limb, three for the crown, seven to down one
  standing, one more than a magazine, so she has to reload in the fight. Hitting a knot while it flares in the
  wind-up still doubles the damage: timing is the reward.
- A **Bellmouth** slam now hits for 2.
- **The Brine grows stronger with each act**: everything above is 8% faster per act (16% at the spring tide), times
  0.85 on Pilgrim and 1.1 on Drowned.

## The Mere's Hollow: an energy bar

The Hollow's resonance is now a real energy bar, shown under the lantern as sea rising in glass.

| Verse | Cost (of 100) | Rings for | Effect now |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Calling | 30 | 4 s | unchanged |
| The Riptide | 35 | 6 s | 130 px cone (from 150), stuns 0.9 s (from 1.6), throws less hard; still tears a grab off her |
| The Salting | 40 | 7 s | crusts a body for 3.5 s (from 6): long enough for one blow, not for a walk round it |
| The Answer | 70 | 30 s | the turned body fights for 10 s (from 16), tearing at one limb until it comes away |

- **How it fills**: a broken knot gives 4, a crown 8. Held in the sea it drinks 2.5 a second, but only to halfway
  (a dotted mark on the bar): the sea alone never fills it. Resting at a bell fills it to halfway, not to the brim.
- **Ringing**: a verse just sung rings in the bone. For 2.5 s the Hollow drinks nothing from the sea (the water in the
  bar trembles), and that verse cannot be sung again until it has rung out (the seconds show beside its name).
- **The sea hears it**: every verse raises the swell, which draws more of the drowned up and the score closer.
- The bar lights the share the verse in hand would spend; if it is too quiet, the reach of that verse is outlined
  in red instead.

## The last night's weapons (Act III)

Both appear only at the spring tide, when Maren takes the Pressure Seal. Neither can be upgraded at a bench. They are
opposites: one is the sea, one is the lamp.

### The Burning Glass

Agathe's spare lamp lens, a disc of Mere-ivory in a brass hood, left for Maren in the Ribvault Bell Room with her red
cloth tied round it and a note: *For Maren, if she comes. It burns what the lamp burns. Count who is still alive.*

- Hold fire: the lantern burns in a line (220 px) like the great lamp. Each knot the beam touches burns (about 3.5 a
  second, the nearest one it can hurt), salt plates crack in the heat, and a fallen body held in it for 1.2 s burns
  and never stands again.
- It drinks 15 oil a second (a full lantern in six), slows her to a walk, and after 3 s the lens is too hot: 2 s to
  cool. Its light is light: everything that hunts by the lamp sees it.
- It is weapon 6 on the keyboard (or the d-pad cycle).

### The Saint's Tine

In the roof of the Gullet Mouth's dome there is a hollow in the bone the exact shape of the reliquary, the **Saint's
Ear**. Letters older than the Abbey are cut round its lip: *QUOD AURIS AUDIT, AURIS FIT*, what the ear hears, the ear
becomes. Before the spring tide it is dry. At the spring tide it runs with brine and hums, and scratched beside it, in
the Abbot's hand: *HERE. AT THE SPRING TIDE. IT DOES NOT HURT.*

Lay the Hollow in it with the Harpoon Gun across it, if she chooses, and the bone grows the two together.

- The Tine throws a lance of the sea itself (640 px/s, about 320 px) that runs through up to **three** bodies and
  breaks a knot in each (3 damage), staggering them. It takes the Harpoon Gun's place in her hands.
- Each throw costs **a quarter of the Hollow** (25). Grown together, the Hollow drinks deeper from the knots it breaks
  (7 a knot, 14 a crown) and the sea fills it **to the brim**.
- **Empty, it drinks from her**: a throw costs a pip of health, and sets the sea's hold forward by 20 s.
- Grown together, the Hollow **sings no verses** (no Calling, no Riptide to tear a grab off her). The bells hurt it:
  in a Bell Room it bleeds the Hollow dry and will not throw, and resting heals her by half.
- Every throw is loud (twelve tiles) and raises the swell.
- A **bench prises it apart** again: the Harpoon Gun comes back as it was, at its tier, and the Hollow comes back
  empty. Once it has rooted (below), prising it out tears her for 2 pips.

## The sea's hold, and the fourth ending

Every second the Tine is grown together outside a Bell Room counts, and the count is kept in the save. Nothing takes it
back except the lamp's own light: firing the Glass while she carries the Tine burns her for a pip as it catches, and
burns 4 s of the hold out of her for every second it burns. A thin vein under the Hollow's bar shows how far it has
gone.

| The hold | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| A quarter (2 min) | *The Tine is warm when I wake. I don't remember picking it up.* The dark talks to her more. |
| Halfway (4 min) | It has **rooted**: prising it out now tears her. The whisper in the dark says *come down*. |
| Three quarters (6 min) | The drowned hesitate before they strike (a quarter-second longer wind-up). Her eyes show sea-green inside the helmet. |
| All the way (8 min) | *I can hear it. Under the island. It is so old, and so tired.* |

The drowned choir follows her everywhere while she carries it, louder as the hold grows, and the islanders each say
something about the thing in her hand the first time they see it. Hesper knows what it is: the Abbot never wanted the
Hollow for his pans. He meant to lay it in the Ear and be grown into what the Saint is, the thing the sea dreams
through. She rowed it past the reef so that no one could.

If the hold is complete and Maren still has the Tine in her hand when she reaches the lamp, Agathe sees it, and the
lamp room's seaward window shows the sea pressed against the glass, impossibly high, faces in it. That is the
**fourth ending, Host of the Mere**: Maren does what the Abbot could not. The old Saint lets go, the drowned turn their
faces toward her, and she takes her mother down into the water with her. The lamp is never lit again, and every
ship the reef takes comes up the Long Pier at High tide singing to her. It has its own epilogue for every islander.
